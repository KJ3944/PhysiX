03

Physics Simulation Engine & Numerical Accuracy

PhysiX utilizes high-precision Newtonian rigid-body physics (Matter.js 2D), discrete integration algorithms, numerical kinematic solvers, and optoelectronic transfer equations. While simulations are calibrated against experimental textbook physics:

  • Discrete Time-Step Limits: High-velocity ballistic trajectories and collision events depend on discrete delta-time ($\Delta t$) stepping and the browser rendering frame rate. Minor integration drift or floating-point variations may occur under extreme boundary parameters.
  • Simplified Models: Certain real-world non-linear perturbations (such as atmospheric turbulence, humidity-dependent air resistance, or non-uniform laser speckle patterns) may be idealized for clarity of foundational physics concepts.
  • No Critical Infrastructure Use: PhysiX models are calibrated for educational and pedagogical training; they must not be used as the sole safety-critical calculations for physical aerospace, structural engineering, or life-critical designs.

Experiment 2: Numerical Aperture of an Optical Fibre Laboratory

Simulates an optoelectronic optical bench designed to determine the Numerical Aperture ($NA$) and Acceptance Angle ($\theta_a$) of a step-index multimode plastic optical fibre (POF) using a laser diode source and a movable graduated screen.

1 Step-by-Step Operations Guide: How to Turn On & Operate the Optical Bench

Step 1
Turn On Main Power

Click the "Main Power" button (⚡ Main Power) in the Trainer Hardware Deck. The green LED will turn ON.

Step 2
Turn On Laser Source

Click the "Laser Source" button (🔴 Laser Source). The coherent laser diode beam will emit through the optical fibre.

Step 3
Select Laser Wavelength

Click any wavelength chip: 650 nm (Red), 532 nm (Green), 405 nm (Violet), or 850 nm (IR).

Step 4
Slide Screen Distance (L)

Drag the Screen Distance (L) slider (or click presets: 2.0 cm, 4.0 cm, 6.0 cm, 8.0 cm, 10.0 cm).

Step 5
Measure Spot Diameter (W)

Observe the divergent cone circular spot size $W$ on the frosted screen. NA is calculated via $NA = \frac{W}{\sqrt{4L^2 + W^2}}$.

Step 6
Record & Export

Click "Record Reading" to log the data row, then click "Export CSV" or "Export PDF" to save reports.

Aim & Scientific Principle

Aim: To determine the Numerical Aperture (NA) and Acceptance Angle (θ_a) of an optical fibre using the output light spot divergence method on a graduated optical bench.

Apparatus: Optical fibre trainer kit, power supply, 650nm red laser diode, step-index optical fibre cable, precision NA measurement jig, white screen with concentric circles (1.0cm to 3.5cm), graduated bench scale.

Experimental NA Formula: NA = W / √(4L² + W²)
Waveguide Refractive Index Law: NA = √(n₁² - n₂²) = sin(θ_a)
Acceptance Cone Half-Angle: θ_a = arcsin(NA)
Mean Numerical Aperture: Mean NA = (1/N) ∑ NAᵢ

Theoretical Foundations

  • Optical Fibre: A cylindrical dielectric waveguide composed of a high-index Core (n₁) surrounded by a lower-index Cladding (n₂), where n₁ > n₂.
  • Total Internal Reflection (TIR): Light rays incident at angles greater than the critical angle θ_c = arcsin(n₂/n₁) undergo total reflection at the core-cladding boundary.
  • Acceptance Cone: The maximum cone of light rays incident on the fiber core face that will be guided through the fiber by TIR.
  • Diverging Output Spot: Upon exiting the fiber tip, the light beam diverges conically with half-angle θ_a, forming a circular spot of diameter W on a screen at distance L.

Aim & Optoelectronic Principle

Aim: To investigate the characteristics of the TCS3200 optoelectronic colour sensor, analyze RGB photodiode spectral responsivities, verify light-to-frequency conversion, and compute normalized chromaticity coordinates.

Apparatus: TCS3200 color sensor module (8×8 photodiode array), 5V DC power supply, 4-element white LED spotlight array, calibrated color swatches (Red, Green, Blue, Yellow, Cyan, Magenta, White, Black), precision Vernier optical standoff rail (5-30 mm), digital oscilloscope/frequency counter.

Photocurrent Generation: I_ph = η × (q P_opt λ) / (h c)
Light-to-Frequency Conversion: f_out ∝ I_ph (Linear Square Wave)
Normalized Chromaticity Coordinates: r = f_R / (f_R + f_G + f_B)
Inverse-Square Distance Law: E(d) = E₀ × (d₀ / d)²

TCS3200 Pin Multiplexer Truth Tables

Filter Selection (S2, S3):
  • S2=0, S3=0: Red Filter (~650nm)
  • S2=0, S3=1: Blue Filter (~470nm)
  • S2=1, S3=0: Clear (Unfiltered 350-950nm)
  • S2=1, S3=1: Green Filter (~540nm)
Frequency Scaling (S0, S1):
  • S0=0, S1=0: Power Down (0 Hz)
  • S0=0, S1=1: 2% Scale (0.1 - 12 kHz)
  • S0=1, S1=0: 20% Scale (1 - 120 kHz, Optimal)
  • S0=1, S1=1: 100% Scale (5 - 600 kHz)

Theoretical Foundations & Colorimetry

  • Photodiode Matrix: The TCS3200 contains an 8×8 array of 64 silicon photodiodes (16 Red, 16 Green, 16 Blue, and 16 Clear). Grouping them symmetrically eliminates non-uniform irradiance errors.
  • White Balance Normalization: Raw frequencies $f_R, f_G, f_B$ are scaled against a standard white reference card ($R_{norm} = (f_R / f_{R,white}) \times 255$) to reconstruct true 24-bit sRGB color.
  • Standoff Distance Effect: At close distances ($d < 8\text{ mm}$), mechanical casing shadows reduce illumination. At larger distances ($d > 15\text{ mm}$), reflected irradiance drops via $1/d^2$, making $d \approx 10-14\text{ mm}$ the ideal optical focus.
